Choosing the right self-tapping screw for light-gauge steel (0.6 – 1.5 mm) comes down to **three questions**: are you fastening into a pre-punched or pre-drilled hole, or not? Is the substrate uniform or multi-layer? And what holding force do you actually need?

## Quick reference

| Substrate | Tip type | Typical thread | Drive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.6–0.9 mm steel | Thread-forming (Type A) | Fine, spaced | Phillips / Torx |
| 1.0–1.5 mm steel | Thread-cutting (Type B/T) | Coarser | Torx preferred |
| Steel-to-aluminium | Thread-cutting (Type B) | Coarse | Torx |
| Multi-layer (steel+wood) | Self-drilling tip + thread-rolling | Coarse | Torx / hex |
| Pre-drilled hole | Machine screw thread | Standard M-class | Phillips / hex |

## Why the tip matters

- **Thread-forming (Type A)** displaces material — good for thin, ductile substrates but can cause stress marks around the joint.
- **Thread-cutting (Type B/T)** actually removes chips — cleaner in multi-layer joints, less driving torque.
- **Self-drilling tips** add a drill point — saves a separate operation but requires close tolerance on perpendicularity.

## Specifying correctly for an OEM

If you are designing a new assembly and want predictable torque-out performance:

1. State the **substrate grade and thickness** (e.g. "DC01, 1.0 mm").
2. State the **minimum pull-out and torque-out values** (typically ≥ 1.5 kN for thin steel).
3. Ask for the **washer face diameter** if the joint is on a soft cladding.
4. Specify the **coating** (zinc flake, zinc-electroplated, zinc-nickel, or stainless A2/A4).
